# Part 1: How it works

Read this part when you get stuck. It has the answer to almost every question you will have.

## Putting it all together

This is the term-ending project. You take the skills you learned in P01 through P08 and put them together into one robot: **the Sumo Bot.**

**WORK 1** is a robot that patrols the ring and turns away from the edge. You build it from P08's patterns, plus the one new idea in this project: the edge guard.

**WORK 2 and WORK 3 are yours to design.** Each one is a new state, built from patterns you already know.

## What you are building

The ring has a black floor and a white rim. Robots drive around inside it. A robot that drives into another one shoves it, and a robot that reaches the white backs off and goes somewhere else. The last robot in the ring wins.

![Three sumo bots patrolling a ring with a white rim](images/p09_ring.png)

## WORK 1: patrol, turn, and the edge guard

Two states, both from P08, and one new idea that connects them.

**PATROLLING, from P08.** This is how P08 wrote its patrol:

```
if current_state == PATROLLING:
    sb.light_both_leds(0, 1, 0)
    alvik.drive(PATROL_SPEED_CMS, 0)

    if distance < SPOT_CM:
        current_state = WARNING
```

Write yours the same way, green, with two changes. Sumo speeds are in RPM, so the wheels use P03's line instead of `drive()`:

```
alvik.set_wheels_speed(left_speed, right_speed)
```

Give both wheels `PATROL_RPM` and the robot goes straight. And leave out the test at the bottom. Your patrol has no way out of its own. Something else sends it to `TURNING`.

**TURNING, from P08.** This is how P08 started to run away:

```
elif current_state == TURNING:
    sb.light_both_leds(0, 0, 1)
    alvik.move(-BACK_UP_CM)
    alvik.rotate(RETREAT_TURN_DEG)
    current_state = RUNNING
```

Write yours the same way: blue light, back up, turn. `BACK_UP_CM` is in your file under the same name. Two things change. The angle is `TURN_DEG`. And there is no `RUNNING`, because patrolling drives you away, so `TURNING` ends by going back to `PATROLLING`.

`TURN_DEG` is 135, P08's angle for P08's reason. A robot that turns right around runs back and forth along the same line forever. A quarter turn runs it along the rim and straight back into it. Change `TURN_DEG` if you like. It decides where your robot goes for the whole match.

## The new idea: a test that outranks the tree

The last piece of WORK 1 is the only thing that sends your robot to `TURNING`. It is two lines: ask `edge_detected()`, and if it is true, set `current_state` to `TURNING`. `edge_detected()` is given at the top of your file. It is true when a line sensor reaches the white rim.

In P08 every transition lived inside a branch, and it took effect on the **next** pass of the loop. A transition is a note to your future self.

The edge cannot wait for the next pass. It cannot live inside a branch either. A robot has to back off the rim whatever state it is in, including the two states you are about to add. Copying the same test into every branch is how programs rot.

So it sits **above the tree**, before the first `if`:

- It runs before any branch is chosen.
- It writes `current_state` while the tree is still ahead of it. The tree sees the new value, so the turning branch runs on **this** pass.
- It beats every branch below it, because it already happened.

That is called a **guard**. It does the one thing P08 told you transitions do not do.

Put the guard where your file says WORK 1: THE EDGE GUARD. The screen line sits **below** that spot, on purpose. The guard sets `TURNING`, and the turning branch sets it back on the same pass. A screen written above the guard would never once show `TURNING`.

## WORK 2 and WORK 3: your two new states

Each one is a new state that does something your robot could not do before. Each one needs four things:

- **A name**, spelled once, at the WORK 2 and WORK 3 comment near the top of your file.
- **A light color** no other state uses, so you can tell it apart from across the room.
- **A branch** at the end of the chain, under `TURNING`, where your file says WORK 2 or WORK 3.
- **A way in.** Usually a test at the bottom of `PATROLLING`, the way P08's patrol spotted the clown.

Pick two from this list, or invent your own.

| Idea | What it does | Patterns |
|---|---|---|
| Charge | Something closer than 3 cm: full speed, 70 RPM, until it is further than 5 cm. | P08's two limits with a gap, P03's wheels |
| Re-aim | After 10 seconds of patrol, turn somewhere new, then patrol again. | P07's clock, P08's `warning_start` |
| Break the standoff | Charging for 3 seconds without moving the other robot: back off and come at it from the side. Needs Charge first. | P08's timer, P04's `move()` and `rotate()` |
| Your own | Anything that makes your robot better at staying in or pushing out. | Say which patterns it uses on your worksheet. |

**Why 3 cm for a charge.** In P08 the sensor saw the classroom floor at about 19 cm. The ring floor is worse: it can fool the sensor from about 5 cm out. So the robot only believes something that is almost touching it.

**Why the gap.** P08 started warning closer than `SPOT_CM` and stopped further than `FLED_CM`. Without that gap, an opponent wobbling at 3 cm flips your robot in and out of the charge in the middle of a shove.

**Why the guard still wins.** At patrol speed the rim gives you a little under a second between touching white and falling out. At 70 RPM it is about six tenths of a second. Your new states do not need their own edge test. The guard already beats every branch, so a charge that shoves an opponent over the rim turns around instead of following it out.

When both of your states are in, finish the chain with P08's catch-all. It goes at the very end, under WORK 3, and it does not change at all:

```
else:
    sb.light_both_leds(1, 0, 0)
    sb.log_info("Bad:", current_state)
    time.sleep_ms(3000)
    break
```

# Part 2: Do the work

## Step 1: Set up

1. Turn on your Alvik robot and connect it to your Mac with the USB cable.
2. Open `p09_sumo_bot.py` in Thonny and save your own copy to the robot as described at the top of this guide. **Name the file** `p09.py`.

Work in the ring.

## Step 2: Write WORK 1

Write the guard at the `# --- WORK 1: THE EDGE GUARD` comment. Write your `PATROLLING` and `TURNING` branches where the `pass` line is, then delete the `pass` line.

Run it. The lights flash white. Hold OK. The robot patrols on green. At the white the screen says `Sumo: TURNING`, the lights go blue, and it backs off, turns, and goes green again. Leave it. It should stay in the ring for as long as you let it run.

**If it drives over the white**, your guard is missing or inside a branch. If your guard is above the tree and it still drives out, `EDGE_THRESHOLD` does not match this ring. Tell Mr. Salemi.

**If it backs off the rim but the screen never shows it**, the screen line has moved above your guard.

**If it stops with a NameError** on `PATROL_SPEED_CMS` or `RETREAT_TURN_DEG`, P09 uses `PATROL_RPM` and `TURN_DEG`.

**If it goes back and forth across the same line**, make `TURN_DEG` smaller.

**If it backs off, turns, and hits the rim again right away**, make `TURN_DEG` bigger.

## Step 3: Write WORK 2

Add your first state: its name, its branch under `TURNING`, and its way in.

Run it, and make your state happen. Watch the screen: it should show your state's name, and the lights should show its color.

**If it stops with a NameError**, a name is spelled differently in two places, or you used a number from an earlier project that P09 does not have. Give it a name at the WORK 2 and WORK 3 comment.

**If it keeps driving but the light stops changing**, a state name inside quotes is spelled differently in two places.

**If your state never happens**, check its way in. Print the reading it depends on.

## Step 4: Write WORK 3

Add your second state the same way, under your WORK 2 branch. Then add the catch-all as the last thing in the chain.

Run it, and make both of your states happen.

**If it stops with Bad on the screen**, the state it names has no branch, or the word is spelled differently in two places.

## Step 5: Worksheet and check off

One run shows everything. Power the robot up with no cable, put it in the ring, and hold OK. It patrols on green and turns blue at the white. Then make each of your two states happen, and say what it does.
