# Part 1: How it works

Read this part when you get stuck. It has the answer to almost every question you will have.

## Putting it all together

This is the term-ending project. You take the skills you learned in P01 through P08 and put them together into one robot: **the Sumo Bot.**

This guide does not print code for you to type. Each part shows you a **pattern** from an earlier project, the way that project taught it. Then it tells you what to build with it. You write the P09 version yourself.

You learn **one new idea** here: the edge guard. Everything else, you have done before.

## What you are building

The ring has a black floor and a white rim. Robots drive around inside it looking for each other. Reach the white and you back off and go somewhere else. Find another robot right in front of you and you put everything you have into shoving it out.

![Three sumo bots patrolling a ring with a white rim](images/p09_ring.png)

Your robot has three states, and each one has its own color:

- `PATROLLING`, green. Drive and look for an opponent.
- `ATTACKING`, red. Full speed into whatever is in front of you.
- `TURNING`, blue. Back off the rim and turn away.

## The patterns you will use

| Pattern | Learned in | Used here for |
|---|---|---|
| A state machine: one variable, one `elif` chain, a catch-all | P08 | the whole robot |
| A branch: actions on top, transitions at the bottom | P08 | every state |
| Wheel speeds in RPM with `set_wheels_speed()` | P03 | patrolling and attacking |
| Two limits with a gap between them | P08 | starting and ending a charge |
| Blocking moves with `move()` and `rotate()` | P04 and P08 | backing off the rim |
| Turn less than half way | P08 | where you go after the rim |

Two pieces are given to you at the top of `p09_sumo_bot.py`:

- `edge_detected()` is true when a line sensor has reached the white rim. You call it. You never read the line sensors yourself.
- `waiting_for_gamepad()` holds the robot still until you press CROSS. Nothing in the match reads the gamepad after that.

## WORK 1: patrolling, turning, and the catch-all

**The pattern: a state, from P08.** This is how P08 wrote its patrol:

```
if current_state == PATROLLING:
    sb.light_both_leds(0, 1, 0)
    alvik.drive(PATROL_SPEED_CMS, 0)

    if distance < SPOT_CM:
        current_state = WARNING
```

Actions on top, the transition at the bottom. Write your `PATROLLING` branch the same way. The light stays green. Three things change.

**The wheels.** Sumo speeds are in RPM, so use P03's line instead of `drive()`:

```
alvik.set_wheels_speed(left_speed, right_speed)
```

Give both wheels `PATROL_RPM` and the robot goes straight.

**The test.** Anything closer than `ATTACK_CM` starts a charge.

**The next state** is `ATTACKING`.

`ATTACK_CM` is 3, and that is not a round number by accident. In P08 the sensor saw the classroom floor at about 19 cm. The ring floor is worse: it can fool the sensor from about 5 cm out. So your robot only believes something that is almost touching it.

**The pattern: the retreat, from P08.** This is how P08 started to run away:

```
elif current_state == TURNING:
    sb.light_both_leds(0, 0, 1)
    alvik.move(-BACK_UP_CM)
    alvik.rotate(RETREAT_TURN_DEG)
    current_state = RUNNING
```

Write your `TURNING` branch the same way: blue light, back up, turn. `BACK_UP_CM` is in your file under the same name. Two things change.

**The angle** is `TURN_DEG`, 135, which is P08's angle for P08's reason. A robot that turns right around runs back and forth along the same line forever. In a ring that means straight across and straight back, and it never meets anybody. A quarter turn has the opposite problem: it runs you along the rim and straight back into it. Change `TURN_DEG` if you like. It decides where your robot goes for the whole match.

**There is no running state.** P08 needed `RUNNING` to get away. You don't, because patrolling drives you away. So `TURNING` ends by going back to `PATROLLING`.

`move()` and `rotate()` **block**, and that is fine here for P08's reason. Your robot has decided to leave the rim. Nothing it could see would change its mind.

Nothing sends your robot to `TURNING` yet. That is WORK 3.

**The pattern: the catch-all, from P08.** It goes at the very end of the chain, and it does not change at all:

```
else:
    sb.light_both_leds(1, 0, 0)
    sb.log_info("Bad:", current_state)
    time.sleep_ms(3000)
    break
```

Every new state you write goes **above** this `else`, the same as in P08.

## WORK 2: the charge

**The pattern: two limits with a gap, from P08.** This is how P08's warning ended:

```
    if distance > FLED_CM:
        current_state = PATROLLING
    elif time.ticks_diff(now, warning_start) > WARNING_MS:
        current_state = TURNING
```

Look at the first test. P08 started warning closer than `SPOT_CM` (10) and stopped further than `FLED_CM` (15). The gap between them kept a clown standing at exactly 10 cm from flicking the robot in and out of the warning.

A shove has the same problem. Write your `ATTACKING` branch like this:

- **The light** is red, and it stays on. P08 flashed its red. Your robot is not warning anybody.
- **The wheels** are P03's line again, both at `ATTACK_RPM`. That is 70, everything the motors have.
- **One way out:** further than `LOST_CM` goes back to `PATROLLING`.

`LOST_CM` is 5, a little further out than `ATTACK_CM`. That is P08's gap. Without it, an opponent wobbling at 3 cm flips your robot between red and green in the middle of a shove. If the floor ever fools your robot into charging empty space, it charges to the rim, and WORK 3 turns it around there.

Why only one way out? A shove that works ends with your opponent over the rim, and you standing on it. Ending that is WORK 3's job.

## The new idea: a test that outranks the tree

In P08 every transition lived inside a branch, and it took effect on the **next** pass of the loop. A transition is a note to your future self.

The edge cannot wait for the next pass. It cannot live inside a branch either. A robot has to back off the rim whether it was patrolling **or** attacking, and copying the same test into every branch is how programs rot.

So it goes **above the tree**, before your first `if`:

- It runs before any branch is chosen.
- It writes `current_state` while the tree is still ahead of it. The tree sees the new value, so the turning branch runs on **this** pass.
- It beats every branch below it, because it already happened.

That is called a **guard**. It does the one thing P08 told you transitions do not do.

Look at where the screen line sits in your file. It is already **below** the spot for your guard, on purpose. The guard sets `TURNING`, and the turning branch sets it back on the same pass. A screen written above the guard would never once show `TURNING`.

## Why the guard has to beat the charge

The rim is about 7.6 cm wide. At patrol speed that is a little under a second between touching white and falling out. At attack speed it is about six tenths of a second. Attack speed is exactly when your robot is least interested in what is under it.

The guard also does a job you would otherwise have to write. Shove an opponent over the rim and **you** are now standing on the rim. The guard fires before your attack branch runs, so you back off instead of following your victim out.

## WORK 3: the guard

Two lines, at the WORK 3 comment above the screen. Ask `edge_detected()`. If it is true, set `current_state` to `TURNING`.

That is all of WORK 3. Your `TURNING` branch from WORK 1 does the rest, on the same pass.

## What three states do not solve

Two robots meet head-on. Both see something inside 3 cm. Both charge. Neither moves.

Nothing you have written breaks that, and it is the most likely thing to happen in a real match. In testing it looks like two robots, both red, both still, with `ATTACKING` on the screen. If the screen says `Bad:`, that is your catch-all, which is a different problem.

You are allowed more states than three. P07's clock and P08's five-second warning are how a state knows how long it has lasted.

# Part 2: Do the work

## Step 1: Set up

1. Turn on your Alvik robot and connect it to your Mac with the USB cable.
2. Open `p09_sumo_bot.py` in Thonny and save your own copy to the robot as described at the top of this guide. **Name the file** `p09.py`.
3. Run it. The lights blink yellow until you pair your controller, the way you did in P03 and P07. Then they blink green until you press CROSS. Press CROSS: the screen reads `Sumo: PATROLLING` and the robot sits still.

Work in the ring.

## Step 2: Write WORK 1

Find the `# --- WORK 1` comment. Write your `PATROLLING` branch and your `TURNING` branch where the `pass` line is, then delete the `pass` line. Write the catch-all at the end of the chain.

Run it in the middle of the ring. Green lights, and the robot drives straight. Nothing stops it at the rim yet, so pick it up before the white.

Put your hand right in front of it. It pushes for three seconds, then stops with `Bad: ATTACKING` on the screen. That is correct. You have not written that branch yet, and the catch-all told you so.

**If it stops with a NameError** on `SPOT_CM` or `RETREAT_TURN_DEG`, P09 calls those numbers `ATTACK_CM` and `TURN_DEG`.

**If it drives in a curve**, your two wheel speeds are not the same.

## Step 3: Write WORK 2

Write your `ATTACKING` branch between your `PATROLLING` branch and the `else`.

Run it. Put your hand right in front of it. It goes red and fast. Pull your hand away and it goes green. Pick it up before the white.

**If it flickers between red and green while your hand is still**, your way out uses `ATTACK_CM`. It should use `LOST_CM`.

**If it stops with a NameError** on `FLASH_MS` or `last_flash`, you brought in P08's flash. P09's red stays on.

## Step 4: Write WORK 3

Write the guard at the `# --- WORK 3` comment, above the screen and above the tree.

Run it in the ring and leave it. It patrols. At the white the screen says `Sumo: TURNING`, the lights go blue, and it backs off, turns, and goes green again. It should stay in the ring for as long as you leave it.

Now test the guard during a charge. Put a box right in front of the robot, aimed at the rim, so it charges toward the white. It has to turn at the rim, the same as when it patrols.

**If it drives over the white**, your guard is missing. If it turns at the rim on patrol but charges straight off, your guard is inside the `PATROLLING` branch. If your guard is above the tree and it still drives out, `EDGE_THRESHOLD` does not match this ring. Tell Mr. Salemi.

**If it backs off the rim but the screen never shows it**, the screen line has moved above your guard. The screen should say `Sumo: TURNING`.

**If it goes back and forth across the same line**, make `TURN_DEG` smaller.

**If it backs off, turns, and hits the rim again right away**, make `TURN_DEG` bigger.

**If it stops with Bad** and `TURNING` on the screen, your `TURNING` branch from WORK 1 is missing, or the word is spelled differently in two places.

## Step 5: Worksheet and check off

One run shows everything. Power the robot up with no cable, put it in the ring, and press CROSS. It patrols on green. At the white it shows `TURNING` in blue, backs off, turns, and carries on. Hold something right in front of it and it goes red and fast. Take it away and it goes green. Let it charge a box to the rim and it turns there.
