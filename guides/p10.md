# Part 1: How it works

Read this part when you get stuck. It has the answer to almost every question you will have.

## What you are building

The board is white, with a black line down the middle. Two robots start at opposite ends and race toward each other. An obstacle sits on the line halfway. Each robot goes around it to the **left**, so two robots that meet head-on pass each other. Then it finds the line again and races to the far end.

In a race your robot drives right off the end of the board. Catch it.

## This time you design every state

P08 gave you its states. P09 gave you two and you designed two more. In P10 every state is yours. You need one state for each thing the robot does:

| WORK | What the robot does |
|---|---|
| WORK 1 | Follows the line. |
| WORK 2 | Goes around the obstacle, to the left. |
| WORK 3 | Finds the line again and follows it. |

Each state needs the same four things as in P09:

- **A name**, spelled once, at the top of your file where it says WORK 1, 2 and 3.
- **A light color** no other state uses.
- **A branch** in the chain.
- **A way in.**

This is how P08 laid out its chain:

```
if current_state == PATROLLING:
    # what a patrolling robot does

elif current_state == WARNING:
    # what a warning robot does

elif current_state == TURNING:
    # and so on
```

Yours has the same shape, with your names. Your file starts with `current_state` empty. Set it to your WORK 1 state.

## The new idea: the robot steers itself

Following a line means steering all the time. A little left, a little right, many times a second. `sb.follow_line()` does that steering for you. Your job is the state around it.

Each pass of the loop, ask it for two wheel speeds and give them to the wheels:

```
left_speed, right_speed = sb.follow_line(DRIVE_RPM)
alvik.set_wheels_speed(left_speed, right_speed)
```

Just before you start following, call this once. Do it at the start, and every time you find the line again. It clears what the steering remembers about the last line it saw:

```
sb.reset_line()
```

When no sensor sees the line, `sb.follow_line()` drives straight. That is why your robot runs off the end of the board.

`DRIVE_RPM` starts at 30, which is safe. Race pace is about 40-45. Keep it at 50 or below.

## WORK 2: going around

Going around is P08's retreat. This is how P08 backed up and turned:

```
elif current_state == TURNING:
    sb.light_both_leds(0, 0, 1)
    alvik.move(-BACK_UP_CM)
    alvik.rotate(RETREAT_TURN_DEG)
    current_state = RUNNING
```

`move()` and `rotate()` make the robot wait until they finish. That is fine here, because nothing needs watching on the way around. A positive angle turns left. A negative angle turns right.

Two shapes to try. Measure your obstacle first.

| Shape | How it goes |
|---|---|
| Square corners | Turn 90 left, drive out, turn 90 right, drive past the obstacle, turn back toward the line. You meet the line straight on. |
| Wide corners | Turn about 45 left, drive past, turn 90 right. You cross the line on a slant. |

The way in is a test in your WORK 1 state: something closer than `OBSTACLE_CM`. The white board can fool the distance sensor into seeing something at about 15 cm, so keep `OBSTACLE_CM` under that. Tune it.

## WORK 3: finding the line

Each pass, your file reads the three line sensors into `left_line`, `center_line` and `right_line`. A line sensor reads **high over black** and **low over white**. A reading above `sb.LINE_THRESHOLD` means that sensor sees black.

Your WORK 3 state drives toward the line until a sensor sees black. Then it clears the steering and goes back to following. Which sensor sees the line first depends on your shape.

When all three of your states are in, finish the chain with P08's catch-all. It goes at the very end, under WORK 3:

```
else:
    sb.light_both_leds(1, 0, 0)
    sb.log_info("Bad:", current_state)
    time.sleep_ms(3000)
    break
```

# Part 2: Do the work

## Step 1: Set up

1. Turn on your Alvik robot and connect it to your Mac with the USB cable.
2. Open `p10_line_racer.py` in Thonny and save your own copy to the robot as described at the top of this guide. **Name the file** `p10.py`.

Work on the racing board.

## Step 2: Write WORK 1

Name your following state at the top of the file, and set `current_state` to it. Write its branch where the `pass` line is, then delete the `pass` line.

Take the obstacle off the board. Put the robot on the line, facing along it. Run it. The lights flash white. Hold OK. The robot follows the line in your color and runs off the end.

**If it drives straight and ignores the line**, check that you call `sb.follow_line()` every pass, and give both speeds to the wheels.

**If it swings off the line on a curve or a wobble**, make `DRIVE_RPM` smaller.

**If the screen shows nothing after OK**, `current_state` is still empty.

**If it stops with a NameError**, a state name is spelled differently in two places.

## Step 3: Write WORK 2

Put the obstacle back. Name your going-around state, write its branch under WORK 1, and add its way in to your WORK 1 state.

Run it. The robot should follow the line, stop short of the obstacle, and go around it to the left.

**If it goes around with nothing in front of it**, `OBSTACLE_CM` is too big. The board is fooling the sensor.

**If it hits the obstacle**, `OBSTACLE_CM` is too small, or your way in is missing.

**If it goes around to the right**, an angle has the wrong sign.

**If it clips the obstacle on the way around**, drive further out before you turn back.

## Step 4: Write WORK 3

Name your finding state, write its branch under WORK 2, and send your WORK 2 state to it. Then add the catch-all as the last thing in the chain.

Run it. The robot should go around, find the line again, and follow it to the end.

**If it drives right over the line**, print the three line sensors as it crosses, and check your test against `sb.LINE_THRESHOLD`.

**If it finds the line and follows it back the wrong way**, come at the line on more of a slant.

**If it stops with Bad on the screen**, the state it names has no branch, or the word is spelled differently in two places.

## Step 5: Worksheet and check off

One run shows everything. Power the robot up with no cable, put it on the line, and hold OK. It follows the line, goes around the obstacle to the left, finds the line, and runs off the end.

## FLEX: The A+

**The ping-pong racer.** Hold Center, the button between the four arrows, instead of OK. Your file sets `ping_pong` to True.

Each end of the line has a black region. In ping-pong mode, when all three line sensors see black, the robot spins around and races back the way it came: along the line, around the obstacle to its left, and on to the other end. Then it does it again, until you press Cancel. Two ping-pong robots can share one line.

Start with the sensors on the line, just past the black. In the FLEX box, write the states you added and the test that leads into each one.
