Ray, three times in one thread: *"You talk too much."* — *"Too much
writing. Sum things up."* — *"Read Ray.md and get back to me."*

**Why:** on 2026-09-04 he asked "can we create exactly 0.2 m/s/s?" The
answer was no, in one sentence, with one number. I sent a table, three
alternatives, a closed-loop design and a risk note. Later he asked whether
two robot folders shared a library structure; I had already answered a
different question at length. Each time the extra material was correct and
verified, which is exactly what made it tempting. Length is not a measure of
effort here — it moves the work of finding the answer onto him.

**How to apply:** the reply to a yes/no question starts with yes or no. If
research turned up something real that he did not ask about, hold it until
the current question is closed, then offer it in one line — do not staple it
to the answer. When he pushes back on length, cut and stop; do not explain
the cut, and do not re-send the same content shorter. A first draft that
feels appropriately thorough is usually already too long.
